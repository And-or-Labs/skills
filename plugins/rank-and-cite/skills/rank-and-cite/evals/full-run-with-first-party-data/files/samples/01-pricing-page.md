# Your pricing page is a spreadsheet with a logo on it

Most pricing pages are built by the person who built the billing system. You can tell. Three columns, eleven rows of checkmarks, and a "Contact us" button where the price should be.

We looked at 212 of them last spring. The median page listed 23 features per tier. The median buyer, in the 14 sales calls we sat in on, asked about three: seats, SSO, and whether they could leave.

So answer those three first. Put the rest behind a toggle. Nobody has ever churned because the comparison table was too short.

One caveat. Enterprise buyers do read the whole table, but they read it in a procurement spreadsheet, not on your site. Export it for them. They'll be grateful, quietly, which is the only way procurement is ever grateful.
